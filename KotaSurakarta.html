About Surakarta

Surakarta City, also known as Solo, is a city in Central Java, Indonesia, with an area of ​​44.04 km2. In 2020, Surakarta had a population of 522,364, with a density of 11,861 people/km2. By the end of 2024, Surakarta's population was projected to reach 589,242.

This city is the third-largest city in southern Java, after Malang in East Java and Bandung in West Java, by population. The eastern side of the city is crossed by the river immortalized in one of the keroncong songs, Bengawan Solo. The city is part of the Greater Solo region, where it serves as the main city.

Along with Yogyakarta, Surakarta was the heir to the Islamic Mataram Kingdom, which was divided through the Treaty of Giyanti in 1755, making Surakarta the residence of Susuhunan Pakubuwana and Adipati Mangkunegara.

History of Surakarta

The city's existence began when Sinuhun Paku Buwana II, king of the Islamic Mataram Sultanate, moved the royal seat from Kartasura to Sala Village, a village not far from the banks of the Bengawan Solo River, after the Kartasura palace was destroyed by a rebel attack. Sunan Pakubuwana II purchased land from the village head of Sala Village, Kyai Sala, for 10,000 ringgit (Dutch guilders) to build a new Mataram palace. Officially, the new Islamic Mataram palace was named the Surakarta Hadiningrat Palace and occupancy began on February 20, 1745.[14] The Giyanti Treaty, signed by Sinuhun Paku Buwana III, the Dutch, and Prince Mangkubumi on February 13, 1755, divided the Mataram region into two: the Kasunanan Surakarta and the Sultanate of Yogyakarta. [15] The territory of the Kasunanan Surakarta further shrank due to the Treaty of Salatiga,held on March 17, 1757, which resulted in Raden Mas Said being recognized as an independent prince with a duchy, called the Mangkunegaran Surakarta Duchy (Pura Mangkunegaran Surakarta). As ruler of Mangkunegaran, Raden Mas Said held the title of Adipati Mangkunegara I.

Cultural Heritage of Surakarta

Surakarta, also known as Solo, is a city rich in cultural heritage and Javanese tradition. The Kraton Kasunanan Surakarta, built in 1745, serves as the royal palace and a center for preserving traditional ceremonies and heirlooms. Another historical landmark, Pura Mangkunegaran, blends Javanese and European architecture and regularly hosts performances of *wayang wong* and gamelan music. The art of *wayang kulit* (shadow puppetry) and *wayang orang* (human puppet performance) thrives in the city, reflecting the deep storytelling tradition of the Javanese people. Music and dance are also integral to Surakarta’s culture, with gamelan ensembles and sacred dances like *Bedhaya Ketawang* symbolizing harmony and spirituality. The city is also renowned for *Batik Solo*, recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage, featuring intricate motifs such as *Sidomukti* and *Parang Kusumo*. Cultural festivals like Sekaten, Solo Batik Carnival, and Grebeg Sudiro showcase the city’s creativity and multicultural spirit. In addition, traditional architecture and historical markets such as Pasar Gede and Loji Gandrung reflect the blend of Javanese, Chinese, and Dutch influences that shape Surakarta’s unique cultural identity.
